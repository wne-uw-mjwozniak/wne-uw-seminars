# Seminar rules

Course `2400-PL3SL339A`, bachelor's diploma seminar, 3 ECTS per semester, pass/fail.
The binding description is the [USOS syllabus](https://usosweb.uw.edu.pl/kontroler.php?_action=katalog2/przedmioty/pokazPrzedmiot&kod=2400-PL3SL339A).

## Format

- The seminar is **remote**. Meetings take place on **Google Meet**, which is also the preferred
  way to reach the supervisor.
- We start with one **joint kick-off**. After that everyone works on their own track,
  individually or in a project team.
- **Every week there is a status check-in.** Progress is tracked on a GitHub Projects kanban board;
  see [kanban-workflow.md](kanban-workflow.md).
- Joint sessions return at two points: when research designs are ready, and when results need
  interpreting. You present; the group gives feedback.
- Questions between meetings go to **GitHub issues** in your project repository, so the answer
  stays next to the work. University e-mail is for formal matters.

## Toolchain

These are learning outcomes of the course, not preferences.

| Area | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Writing | LaTeX only (Overleaf or a local TeX distribution). Bibliography in BibTeX. |
| Version control | git, hosted on GitHub: thesis sources and analysis code, with a readable history. |
| Language | Python preferred; R accepted. |
| Environment | Python: `uv` with `pyproject.toml`. R: `renv`. |
| Standard | Anyone with access to the repository can recreate your results without contacting you. |

Expected layout of a project repository:

```text
my-thesis/
  README.md         how to reproduce everything
  pyproject.toml    environment definition (uv); uv.lock committed
  data/             raw data, or a script that fetches it
  src/              reusable code
  experiments/      configurations and run scripts
  results/          generated tables and figures
  paper/            LaTeX sources and .bib (start from templates/thesis/)
```

- One command rebuilds the results; one command rebuilds the PDF.
- Figures and tables in the thesis are generated by code, never pasted by hand.
- Fix random seeds. Record data versions and download dates.
- If data cannot be shared, document how to obtain it and commit a small synthetic sample.
- No secrets, API keys or personal data in git.

## Passing

**After semester 1:** thesis outline in LaTeX; data collected; research questions and hypotheses;
literature review started; git repository with thesis sources and analysis code.

**After semester 2:** literature review and modelling complete; thesis ready for defence; code
reproduces all results from the repository; for high-potential work, a draft of a scientific article.

In both semesters you need **documented progress** (commits, successive versions of the document)
and **active participation** in consultations and joint discussions. A board with no movement for
weeks is the main risk signal.

## Proposing a topic

First message, four things:

1. A working title.
2. Two or three sentences: whose problem is it, what do they want to achieve, what is missing today?
3. Confirmation that the data exists and you can get it.
4. Your first name and surname exactly as in USOS, your student (album) number and your programme
   of study. The supervisor needs these to submit the topic, and the Dean's Office rejects forms
   with swapped names or wrong numbers.

Then a **two-page concept note**: the problem; why it matters to an economist or a financial
decision-maker; research questions and hypotheses; methods and data; planned structure. The concept
note later grows into your introduction.

You can bring your own idea or join one of the projects in [research-topics.md](research-topics.md).

## Teams and articles

Ambitious projects are encouraged to take the **form of a scientific article**, and larger research
programmes can be split into several theses that combine into one journal submission. Faculty rules
allow at most two student authors per thesis, so each thesis in a larger project must be a clearly
separable part with its own question and contribution. Authorship of the resulting paper follows
contribution, agreed and written down at the start. Details: [wne-thesis-rules.md](wne-thesis-rules.md).

## AI tools

The faculty requires a signed declaration on the use of AI systems in every thesis. In this seminar:

- Use AI like a capable colleague: code, debugging, language, critique. Not to produce the
  research idea, the interpretation or the conclusions.
- You must be able to explain every line of code and every sentence. Expect to be asked.
- Verify every reference. Citing a paper that does not exist is a disqualifying error.
- Keep a short log of what you used AI for; the declaration then writes itself.

## Writing standards

- The logic is a chain: problem, hypotheses, methods and data, structure. A chapter that does not
  help answer the question does not belong.
- Every chapter opens with what it does and why, and closes with what was established and how it
  leads to the next one.
- Short sentences, precise words. Claims need evidence: a citation or your own result.
- Every figure and table is referenced by number, interpreted in the text, and has a source.
- Conclusions: your results, limitations, practical use, future work.
- The abstract must make sense to someone who will never read the thesis.
