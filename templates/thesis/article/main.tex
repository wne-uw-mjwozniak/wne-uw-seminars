\section{Introduction}
State the problem, why it matters and to whom, what is missing in the literature, and your
contribution. End with the structure of the paper. Cite in author--year style:
\citet{bollerslev1986garch} introduced GARCH; long memory in realised volatility is captured
parsimoniously by the HAR model \citep{corsi2009har}; pretrained time-series models such as
Chronos \citep{ansari2024chronos} are a recent alternative.

\section{Related literature}
Organise by idea, not by paper. Close each strand by stating what it leaves open.

\section{Data}
Sources, sample period, frequency, cleaning steps, descriptive statistics. Say exactly how the
data can be obtained; the repository must be able to rebuild every number from it.

\section{Methodology}
Models, benchmarks, estimation, and the evaluation protocol: data splits, rolling or expanding
windows, loss functions, statistical tests. Define all notation once:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha \varepsilon_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2 .
  \label{eq:garch}
\end{equation}
